The formulas used in this whole problem set

A loudspeaker below a few hundred Hz behaves like one mass-spring-damper, driven by the voice coil. Five numbers (the Thiele-Small parameters) describe it:
Resonance frequency (mass and suspension spring):
Mechanical Q (how much the suspension damps the resonance):
Electrical Q (how much the coil and amplifier damp it: the back-EMF drives a current through R_E that brakes the cone):
Total Q (both dampings together):
Equivalent volume (the air volume that is as stiff as the suspension):
Symbols: M_MS moving mass including the air [kg], C_MS suspension compliance [m/N], R_MS suspension damping [Ns/m], Bl force factor [Tm], R_E coil resistance [ohm], S_D cone area [m^2].

Problem 3.1 - Efficiency

Efficiency = acoustic power out / electrical power in. Above resonance the cone is mass controlled and the coil impedance is about R_E. Working it through (lecture slide 18), the frequency cancels and what is left is

Problem 4 - The loudspeaker model over frequency

Problem 4 asks for this in LTspice (the finished model is in LTspice/Problems 7 - Loudspeaker/). Here we compute the same three curves: input impedance, sound pressure and displacement.
Step 1: the air load. Each side of the cone pushes on air. For a piston in a baffle at low frequency that air acts as a mass per side. Seen from the cone it adds to the mass.
The data sheet's 88.2 g already includes that air. The model adds the air separately, so we need the mass WITHOUT air, the diaphragm mass:
a = sqrt(S_D/pi)
a = 0.1285
M_A1 = 8*rho/(3*pi^2*a)
M_A1 = 2.5237
M_MD = M_MS - 2*S_D^2*M_A1
M_MD = 0.0746
M_MD_gram = M_MD*1000
M_MD_gram = 74.6286
Step 2: the full air load (not just the mass). At higher frequency the air load also has a resistive part, the part that actually radiates sound. Leach's circuit for a baffled piston is
with these element values:
R_A1 = 0.441*rho*c/S_D
R_A1 = 3.5107e+03
R_A2 = rho*c/S_D
R_A2 = 7.9609e+03
C_A1 = 5.94*a^3/(rho*c^2)
C_A1 = 8.8702e-08
Step 3: the frequency axis (5 Hz to 20 kHz, 2000 points, log spaced) and .
f = logspace(log10(5), log10(20000), 2000);
jw = 1j*2*pi*f;
The air-load circuit from step 2, typed in (a parallel connection is 1/(1/Z1 + 1/Z2)):
Z_ar = 1 ./ ( 1./(jw*M_A1) + 1./(R_A2 + 1./(1/R_A1 + jw*C_A1)) );
Step 4: mechanical impedance of the moving cone. Mass, damper, spring, plus the air load on both sides transformed by S_D^2:
Z_M = jw*M_MD + R_MS + 1./(jw*C_MS) + 2*S_D^2*Z_ar;
Step 5: what the amplifier sees. The coil is a gyrator: the mechanical impedance shows up at the terminals as (Bl)^2 / Z_M:
Z_E = R_E + Bl^2./Z_M;
Step 6: from 1 V to cone velocity, volume velocity, pressure and displacement.
e_g = 1;
i = e_g./Z_E;
u = Bl*i./Z_M;
U = S_D*u;
p_far = jw*rho.*U/(2*pi*1);
p_near = Z_ar.*U;
x = u./jw;

Summary

  • f_S, Q_MS, Q_ES, Q_TS and V_AS all come from M_MS, C_MS, R_MS, Bl, R_E and S_D.
  • The electrical damping usually dominates, so Q_TS is close to Q_ES.
  • Free air has half the air mass of a baffle, so f_S is higher there.
  • Above f_S the pressure is flat at (rho/2pi) Bl S_D/(R_E M_MS) per volt.
  • Efficiency is below 1 %; the rest heats the coil.
  • Displacement grows 4x for every octave lower: bass is limited by excursion, treble by heat.
  • In a model that includes the air load, use the mass without air, M_MD.
