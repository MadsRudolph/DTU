2 - The five-layer wall (slides 4-9)

Plane wave, normal incidence. Layers in the order of the sketch on slide 4: drywall 1 cm, insulation 3 cm, brick 5 cm, air gap 1 cm, wood 2 cm. Everything is per unit area: "pressure" is the across variable, particle velocity the through variable, impedances are specific (Pa s/m).

Each element is a two-port (ABCD) matrix, and a chain is just their product:
The last formula is the transmission the slides plot: output pressure over the (ideal) source pressure.
Transfer matrix of a layer (slide 3, the "2-port"). A uniform layer is a lossless transmission line with , :
(In LTspice that is the T element with , , as on slide 6.)
ser = @(Z) [1 Z; 0 1];
shu = @(Y) [1 0; Y 1];
tml = @(w, i) [cos(w*l_l(i)/c_l(i)), 1j*rho_l(i)*c_l(i)*sin(w*l_l(i)/c_l(i)); 1j*sin(w*l_l(i)/c_l(i))/(rho_l(i)*c_l(i)), cos(w*l_l(i)/c_l(i))];
tr = @(T) 1/(T(1,1) + T(1,2)/Rout);
The three models of slides 5-6, as functions of omega:
LEM = @(w) tr(ser(1j*w*M1)*shu(1j*w*C2)*ser(1j*w*M3)*shu(1j*w*C4)*ser(1j*w*M5));
MIXED = @(w) tr(ser(1j*w*M1)*tml(w,2)*ser(1j*w*M3)*tml(w,4)*ser(1j*w*M5));
TMM = @(w) tr(tml(w,1)*tml(w,2)*tml(w,3)*tml(w,4)*tml(w,5));
