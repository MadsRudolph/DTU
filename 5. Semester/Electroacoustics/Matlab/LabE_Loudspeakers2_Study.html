The data are in the labs repo (Labs/Lab E/data/). Each file holds fn (frequency) and H = p/V in Pa/V (pressure at the UMIK over the sound card's line-out voltage). The system files hold three columns of H: woofer, midrange, tweeter, measured in ONE run.

No phase here, on purpose. The UMIK is a USB microphone with its own clock, and every run starts with a random delay between the sound card's output and the microphone. So the absolute phase of a single run means nothing. (In Part 2 the three units are measured in ONE run, so their phase DIFFERENCES are valid.) Say this once in the quiz: the graders want phase on every complex quantity, unless you explain why not.
Three things to see:
  1. Below about 150 Hz all three fall together: that is the driver's own resonance and the anechoic chamber running out, not the baffle.
  2. The small box sits about 6 dB under the screens at 250-600 Hz (the baffle step, next section).
  3. The circular screen has deep, regular dips (1.06 and 2.1 kHz); the IEC screen is much smoother.

Part 1 - The baffle step

A source in a big baffle radiates into half space (2 pi); the baffle acts like a mirror, adding an image source right on top of the real one. For the same cone movement (volume velocity) that doubles the pressure: +6 dB compared with a source radiating into full space (4 pi). At low frequency the small box's front is much smaller than the wavelength, so it is "no baffle", while the screens are "a baffle": we expect up to 6 dB between them.
k = f > 250 & f < 600;
step_dB = mean(20*log10(abs(cir0.H(k)))) - mean(20*log10(abs(box0.H(k))))
step_dB = 6.2070
k = f > 3000 & f < 8000;
high_dB = mean(20*log10(abs(cir0.H(k)))) - mean(20*log10(abs(box0.H(k))))
high_dB = -0.4166
About 6 dB at 250-600 Hz (5.7 dB averaged over both screens), about 0 dB above 3 kHz: at high frequency even the small box's front is many wavelengths wide, so it is a baffle too.

Part 1 - Circular screen: where the dips come from

Every point on the screen's edge re-radiates the wave that reaches it, with inverted sign. With the driver in the centre, every edge point is a = 0.30 m from the driver, so all those edge waves arrive at the mic TOGETHER, a path difference Delta behind the direct wave:
a = 0.30
a = 0.3000
Delta = a + sqrt(a^2 + r^2) - r
Delta = 0.3184
The edge wave is inverted, so it ADDS when it is half a wavelength late and CANCELS when it is a whole wavelength late:

Find the measured first dip:

1059 Hz against 1078 Hz predicted (2 %), and the second dip at 2115 Hz against 2156. At 30 deg the edge points are no longer equally far from the mic, the edge waves arrive spread out in time, and the ripple smears out. On the IEC screen the driver is off-centre: the edges are between 0.20 and 0.44 m away, so the edge waves never line up. That is why the IEC screen is the standard baffle for measuring a driver.

Part 1 - diffrac.m: the circle as a model

diffrac.m (Terai's method) puts a line of edge sources along the baffle's outline and adds their contributions to the direct sound. The circle is given as a polygon with many corners. The source is either a point (a tiny radius) or a piston with the driver's radius (rule of thumb for a 3" driver: a = 3 cm).
It draws its own figures; those (and only those) are closed again.
th = linspace(0, 2*pi, 200)';
corners = [a*cos(th) a*sin(th)];
ours = findobj('Type', 'figure');
[~, ~, fd, Hpoint] = diffrac(corners, r, [0 0; 30 0], 0.001);
fs = 44100
[~, ~, ~, Hpiston] = diffrac(corners, r, [0 0; 30 0], 0.03);
fs = 44100
close(setdiff(findobj('Type', 'figure'), ours))
diffrac returns dB "re free field": an infinite baffle would be +6 dB everywhere. The measured curve also contains the driver's own response, so it is shifted to the model's mean level over 300 Hz - 5 kHz before comparing (only the SHAPE is compared):
km = f > 300 & f < 5000; kd = fd > 300 & fd < 5000;
shift_0 = mean(Hpiston(kd,1)) - mean(20*log10(abs(cir0.H(km))))

shift_30 = mean(Hpiston(kd,2)) - mean(20*log10(abs(cir30.H(km))))
shift_30 = 2.2939

  • The dips line up with the measurement: the model is right about WHERE the diffraction is.
  • The point source keeps rippling at full strength to 20 kHz. The piston ripples less and less above a few kHz, and so does the measurement. A piston with ka > 1 sends little sound sideways along the baffle, so the edges get less to diffract.
  • At 30 deg the piston model falls off above ~8 kHz: that is the piston's own directivity (next section).

Part 1 - IEC screen: directivity against a piston

Divide each off-axis curve by the 0 deg curve. The driver and the baffle are in both, so they cancel, and what is left is how the driver beams. A rigid piston in a baffle beams like:
a_s = 0.03 % 3" driver: radius about 3 cm
a_s = 0.0300
x60 = 2*pi*f/c * a_s * sind(60);
D60 = 2*besselj(1, x60) ./ x60;
x30 = 2*pi*f/c * a_s * sind(30);
D30 = 2*besselj(1, x30) ./ x30;
figure('Name', 'labE_fig3_iec_directivity');
semilogx(f, 20*log10(abs(iec15.H./iec0.H)), f, 20*log10(abs(iec30.H./iec0.H)), f, 20*log10(abs(iec60.H./iec0.H)), 'LineWidth', 1.3); hold on;
set(gca, 'ColorOrderIndex', 2);
semilogx(f, 20*log10(abs(D30)), '--', f, 20*log10(abs(D60)), '--', 'LineWidth', 1.3);
grid on; xlim([300 18000]); ylim([-40 8]);
xlabel('Frequency (Hz)'); ylabel('off-axis / on-axis (dB)');
legend('15\circ', '30\circ', '60\circ', 'piston 30\circ', 'piston 60\circ', 'Location', 'southwest');
title('IEC screen: measured beaming against a rigid piston, a = 3 cm');
Where does the piston reach -3 dB, and its first null? 2J1(x)/x = 0.707 at x = 1.62, and the first null is at x = 3.83:
f_3dB_60 = 1.62 * c / (2*pi*a_s*sind(60))
f_3dB_60 = 3.4059e+03
f_null_60 = 3.83 * c / (2*pi*a_s*sind(60))
f_null_60 = 8.0522e+03
Measured at 60 deg: -3 dB at 3.45 kHz, null at 7.96 kHz. The rigid piston is spot on. At 15-30 deg the measured nulls come EARLY above 10 kHz: up there the cone no longer moves as one rigid piece (break-up), so its effective size and shape change.

What the curves say about the crossover:
  • The tweeter is about 9 dB louder than the midrange in its band, so the crossover must attenuate it by about 9 dB.
  • The tweeter and the midrange cross near 950 Hz, but the tweeter's resonance is at 1.45 kHz (Lab D impedance). Crossing at 950 Hz would drive it at and below its resonance, with large excursion. A crossover around 2.5-3 kHz keeps an octave of margin. DALI's own crossover for this box is at 2.6 kHz.
  • The woofer to midrange crossover goes well below 1.5 kHz (the woofer beams above that, and has break-up peaks at 2.5-3 kHz).

Part 2 - Phase: removing the delay

Each measured phase contains three things:
The delay tau is a phase that grows linearly with f and wraps hundreds of times. The course's phaseunwrap.m removes it with a distance d, then unwraps:
(phaseunwrap.m uses c = 345 and draws into the current figure, so here the same formula is typed in.) One d is used for ALL three units, found as the value that makes the three phases flattest. Here d = -1.15 m: negative, because the card delay in this run was SHORTER than the flight time, so "distance" here is not a physical distance.
d = -1.15
d = -1.1500
phi_w = rad2deg(unwrap(angle(Hw) + 2*pi*fs*d/345));
phi_m = rad2deg(unwrap(angle(Hm) + 2*pi*fs*d/345));
k_t = fs > 515; % the tweeter is only noise below ~500 Hz
phi_t = rad2deg(unwrap(angle(Ht(k_t)) + 2*pi*fs(k_t)*d/345));
Unwrapping fixes the phase only up to whole turns (360 deg). Shift the woofer and the tweeter by whole turns so that all three sit close together at 1 kHz; that changes nothing physical.
phi_w = phi_w - 360*round((interp1(fs, phi_w, 1000) - interp1(fs, phi_m, 1000))/360);
phi_t = phi_t - 360*round((interp1(fs(k_t), phi_t, 1000) - interp1(fs, phi_m, 1000))/360);
Why the tweeter phase goes wild below 500 Hz: there it is 30-50 dB down, in the noise, and the phase of noise is random. unwrap works upwards from the lowest frequency, so every random jump there adds a whole turn (360 deg) to the ENTIRE rest of the curve. Unwrap only inside the band where the unit actually plays.
The cleaner way: the RELATIVE phase between two units in the same run. The delay is the same for both, so it cancels, and nothing needs to be unwrapped or compensated:
rel_wm = rad2deg(angle(Hw ./ Hm));
rel_tm = rad2deg(angle(Ht ./ Hm));
at300 = [interp1(fs, rel_wm, 300) interp1(fs, rel_tm, 300)]
at300 = 1×2

  • Woofer - midrange: -12 deg at 300 Hz. They are nearly in phase where they will cross, with the woofer's acoustic centre a little further back.
  • Tweeter - midrange: +163 deg at 300 Hz, +76 deg at 3 kHz. Below its resonance a tweeter acts as a second-order high-pass, which gives up to +180 deg. Near the crossover it is about 90 deg ahead.
  • So the crossover cannot just assume the units are in phase: the filter orders and the tweeter's polarity are chosen against THIS curve, so that the two outputs add instead of cancelling around 2.6 kHz.

What goes to the project

  • The magnitude AND phase of each unit at the listening position (0 deg), in one run, for the crossover design.
  • The tweeter needs about 9 dB of attenuation; the mid/tweeter crossover belongs around 2.5-3 kHz; the woofer hands over well below 1.5 kHz.
  • Below about 100 Hz Lab E is not valid (chamber, plugged vent): the woofer box's low end comes from Lab D's near-field measurements.
