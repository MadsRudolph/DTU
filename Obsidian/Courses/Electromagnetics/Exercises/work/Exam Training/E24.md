## Q1 — Wave Frequency

![[Q1.png|400]]

> A wave is shown with a known period or wavelength. Determine its frequency.

### Theory  
Frequency is defined as  
$$ f = \frac{1}{T} $$  
where $T$ is the period.  
If the plot shows a full cycle over a certain time interval, measure $T$ directly.  
If instead the spatial wavelength is shown, use the wave speed $v$:  
$$ f = \frac{v}{\lambda}. $$  
On MCQs, the trick is identifying whether the axis is **time** or **space**.  

### Answer Choices  
- [ ] 500 Hz  
- [ ] 250 Hz  
- [ ] 125 Hz  
- [ ] 1 kHz

## Q2 — Phase Velocity from Two Measurements

![[Q2.png|400]]

> Two measurements of the same wave at different times/positions are given. Find the phase velocity.

### Theory  
Phase velocity is:  
$$ v_p = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t_{\text{phase shift}}}. $$  
You identify two points on two snapshots where the wave exhibits the **same phase** (same shape, same point on the cycle).  
Often a plot shows the wave at $t_1$ and $t_2$; find how far the phase crest moved.  

### Answer Choices  
- [ ] 4 km/s  
- [ ] 1 km/s  
- [ ] 2 km/s  
- [ ] 10 m/s

## Q3 — Electrical Length from Phase Delay

![[Q3_img1.png|200]]

> A TL shows a phase delay per unit length. Compute the electrical length $\ell/\lambda$.

### Theory  
The electrical length is:  
$$ \theta = \beta \ell = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\ell. $$  
If the line introduces a phase shift $\Delta \phi$, then:  
$$ \frac{\ell}{\lambda} = \frac{\Delta \phi}{2\pi}. $$  
Convert degrees to radians if needed.  

### Answer Choices  
- [ ] 0.4  
- [ ] 0.2  
- [ ] 0.1  
- [ ] 0.05

## Q4 — Coaxial Cable Parameter Comparison



> Two coax cables have different geometry; determine which parameter differs.

### Theory  
Characteristic impedance:  
$$ Z_0 = \frac{60}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_r}} \ln\!\left(\frac{b}{a}\right). $$  
Propagation constant:  
$$ \gamma = \alpha + j\beta. $$  
Phase constant:  
$$ \beta = \frac{\omega}{v_p}. $$  
Phase velocity:  
$$ v_p = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon}}. $$  
Changes in radius ratio affect $Z_0$; material changes affect $v_p$ and $\beta$.  

### Answer Choices  
- [ ] The characteristic impedance  
- [ ] The propagation constant  
- [ ] The phase constant  
- [ ] The phase velocity

## Q5 — Reflection Coefficient at Plane B

![[Q5.png|500]]

> A load containing R and L is attached to a TL. Compute $\Gamma_B$.

### Theory  
Reflection coefficient at load:  
$$ \Gamma_B = \frac{Z_L - Z_0}{Z_L + Z_0}. $$  
For inductive loads:  
$$ Z_L = R + j\omega L. $$  
Magnitude and angle come from converting to polar form.  

### Answer Choices

- [ ] Γ\_B = 0.50 ∠ 180°  
- [ ] Γ\_B = 0.84 ∠ 60°  
- [ ] Γ\_B = 1.0 ∠ 62°

## Q6 — Reflection Coefficient at Plane A (Cascaded Lines)

![[Q6.png|500]]

> Two transmission line segments are cascaded. Determine $\Gamma_A$.

### Theory  
If the TL is lossless:  
$$ \Gamma_A = \Gamma_B e^{-j2\beta \ell}. $$  
Electrical length determines rotation on the Smith chart.  
Magnitude stays the same; angle shifts by $-2\beta\ell$.  

### Answer Choices

- [ ] Γ\_A = 0.50 ∠ 0°  
- [ ] Γ\_A = 1.0 ∠ 180°

## Q7 — VSWR of a Short-Circuited Lossless TL



> Compute VSWR when a shorted TL is used.

### Theory  
Short-circuit:  
$$ Z_L = 0 \Rightarrow \Gamma = -1. $$  
Then  
$$ \text{VSWR} = \frac{1+|\Gamma|}{1-|\Gamma|}. $$  
If $|\Gamma| = 1$, denominator is zero → VSWR is infinite.  

### Answer Choices  
- [ ] -1  
- [ ] 0  
- [ ] 1  
- [ ] ∞

## Q10 — Two Quarter-Wave Lines in Parallel

![[Q10.png|500]]

> Two λ/4 lines, each transforming a load, are placed in parallel.

### Theory  
Quarter-wave inverter:  
$$ Z_{\text{in}} = \frac{Z_0^2}{Z_L}. $$  
Parallel combination:  
$$ Z_{\parallel} = \left( \frac{1}{Z_{\text{in1}}} + \frac{1}{Z_{\text{in2}}} \right)^{-1}. $$  
If identical:  
$$ Z_{\parallel} = \frac{Z_{\text{in}}}{2}. $$  

### Answer Choices  
- [ ] $Z_0^2 / Z_L$  
- [ ] $Z_L/2$  
- [ ] $Z_0^2 / (2 Z_L)$  
- [ ] $Z_L$

## Q11 — Stub Inductance Design


> Convert required reactance into a short- or open-circuited stub length.

### Theory  
Stub reactance:  
- Short stub:  
  $$ Z_{\text{stub}} = j Z_0 \tan(\beta \ell). $$  
- Open stub:  
  $$ Z_{\text{stub}} = -j Z_0 \cot(\beta \ell). $$  
Inductive means positive reactance.  
Solve for $\ell$:  
$$ \ell = \frac{1}{\beta}\arctan\!\left(\frac{X}{Z_0}\right). $$  

### Answer Choices  
- [ ] 68.0 mm  
- [ ] 20.16 mm  
- [ ] 32.4 mm  
- [ ] 44.9 mm
