## Q1 — Wave Frequency

![[Q1.png|400]]

> The amplitude of a wave is shown as a function of time in the figure above.  
> **What is the frequency of the wave?**

### Theory  
The **period** $T$ is the time it takes for one full cycle. From the graph you read off the distance between two identical points on the waveform (e.g. peak–to–peak).  

The **frequency** is then  
$$
f = \frac{1}{T}.
$$  

If, for example, one period corresponds to $\Delta t = 4\ \mathrm{ms}$, then  
$$
f = \frac{1}{4\cdot10^{-3}} = 250\ \text{Hz}.
$$  

### Solution
From the graph: one complete cycle takes **4 ms** (peak at t=0 to peak at t=4ms).
$$T = 4\ \text{ms} \quad\Rightarrow\quad f = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{1}{4 \times 10^{-3}} = 250\ \text{Hz}$$

### Answer Choices  
- [ ] 500 Hz  
- [x] 250 Hz ✓
- [ ] 125 Hz  
- [ ] 1 kHz

## Q2 — Phase Velocity from Two Measurements

![[Q2.png|400]]

> The amplitude of a wave is measured as a function of time at **two different positions** distanced **1 m apart**. The measurements are shown in the figure above. Assume the distance is **less than one wavelength**.  
> **What is the phase velocity of the wave?**

### Theory
The fundamental definition of phase velocity $u_p$ relates angular frequency $\omega$, phase constant $\beta$, wavelength $\lambda$, and period $T$:

$$
u_p = \frac{\omega}{\beta} = \frac{\lambda}{T} = \lambda f
$$

Practically, this is equivalent to measuring the time shift $\Delta t$ required for a specific phase point (like a crest) to travel a known physical distance $\Delta x$:
$$
u_p = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}
$$

### Solution
Using the corrected measurement from the graph where the time shift is **1 ms**:

Given:
* $\Delta x = 1$ m
* $\Delta t = 1 \text{ ms} = 1 \times 10^{-3}$ s

$$
u_p = \frac{1\ \text{m}}{1 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{s}} = 1000\ \text{m/s} = 1\ \text{km/s}
$$

### Answer Choices
- [ ] 4 km/s
- [x] 1 km/s ✓
- [ ] 2 km/s
- [ ] 10 m/s

## Q3 — Electrical Length from Phase Delay


> A signal is transmitted through a **matched transmission line (TL)**. A phase delay of  
> $$
 \Delta\phi = \frac{\pi}{5}
 $$  
> is measured between the input and output of the TL.  
> **What is the electrical length $\ell/\lambda$ of the TL?**

### Theory  
For a lossless TL,
$$
\beta = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}, \qquad \theta = \beta\ell,
$$  
where $\theta$ is the **phase shift** across length $\ell$.  

We are given $\theta = \Delta\phi$ so  
$$
\Delta\phi = \beta \ell = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,\ell
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\frac{\ell}{\lambda} = \frac{\Delta\phi}{2\pi}.
$$  

### Solution
$$\frac{\ell}{\lambda} = \frac{\Delta\phi}{2\pi} = \frac{\pi/5}{2\pi} = \frac{1}{10} = 0.1$$

### Answer Choices  
- [ ] 0.4  
- [ ] 0.2  
- [x] 0.1 ✓

## Q4 — Coaxial Cable Parameter Comparison

> Which wave parameter is **not** the same for two coaxial cables consisting of the **same materials**, **same outer conductor radius**, but **different inner conductor radius**?

![[Table_2-2.png]]
### Theory  
For a coaxial line, the **characteristic impedance** is  
$$
Z_0 = \frac{60}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_r}} \ln\!\left(\frac{b}{a}\right),
$$  
where $a$ is the inner conductor radius and $b$ is the outer conductor radius.  

- Changing $a$ (inner radius) **changes $\ln(b/a)$**, hence changes $Z_0$.  
- The **phase velocity** $v_p = 1/\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon}$ depends only on material properties, not geometry.  
- The **propagation constant** $\gamma = \alpha + j\beta$ and **phase constant** $\beta$ also depend on material parameters and frequency, not on $a$ if loss is negligible.  

### Solution
Only $Z_0$ depends on the geometry ratio $\ln(b/a)$. All other parameters depend only on material properties.

### Answer Choices  
- [x] The characteristic impedance ✓
- [ ] The propagation constant  
- [ ] The phase constant  
- [ ] The phase velocity

## Q5 — Reflection Coefficient at Plane B

![[Q5.png|500]]

> Consider the TL circuit sketched above consisting of a transmission line terminated with a **series connection of a resistor and an inductor**.
> The TL circuit is operated at a frequency of **100 MHz**.
> **What is the reflection coefficient seen by the TL at reference (ref.) plane B?**

### Theory
1. Compute the load impedance $Z_L = R + j\omega L$, with $\omega = 2\pi f$.
2. For a line with characteristic impedance $Z_0$, the reflection coefficient at the load is
   $$\Gamma_B = \frac{Z_L - Z_0}{Z_L + Z_0}$$

### Solution
Given:
* $f = 100$ MHz $= 10^8$ Hz
* $Z_0 = 75\ \Omega$
* $R = 25\ \Omega$
* $L = 200$ nH $= 200 \times 10^{-9}$ H

**Step 1: Calculate Load Impedance ($Z_L$)**
$$\omega = 2\pi \times 10^8 \approx 6.283 \times 10^8 \text{ rad/s}$$
$$X_L = \omega L = (6.283 \times 10^8) \times (200 \times 10^{-9}) \approx 125.66\ \Omega$$
$$Z_L = 25 + j125.66\ \Omega$$

**Step 2: Calculate Reflection Coefficient ($\Gamma_B$)**
$$\Gamma_B = \frac{Z_L - Z_0}{Z_L + Z_0} = \frac{(25 + j125.66) - 75}{(25 + j125.66) + 75}$$
$$\Gamma_B = \frac{-50 + j125.66}{100 + j125.66}$$

Converting to polar form for division:
* Numerator: $\sqrt{(-50)^2 + 125.66^2} \angle \text{atan2}(125.66, -50) \approx 135.24 \angle 111.7^\circ$
* Denominator: $\sqrt{100^2 + 125.66^2} \angle \text{atan2}(125.66, 100) \approx 160.61 \angle 51.5^\circ$

$$|\Gamma_B| = \frac{135.24}{160.61} \approx 0.84$$
$$\angle \Gamma_B = 111.7^\circ - 51.5^\circ \approx 60.2^\circ$$

$$\Gamma_B \approx 0.84\angle60^\circ$$

### Answer Choices
- [ ] $\Gamma_B = 0.52\angle172^\circ$
- [ ] $\Gamma_B = 0.50\angle180^\circ$
- [x] $\Gamma_B = 0.84\angle60^\circ$ ✓
- [ ] $\Gamma_B = 1.0\angle62^\circ$

$$
\Gamma_A = \Gamma_B e^{-j2\beta\ell},
$$  
where $\beta = 2\pi/\lambda$.

$$\Gamma_L = \frac{25 - 75}{25 + 75} = -0.5$$

Since $\ell = 5\lambda$, the phase rotation is $2\beta\ell = 2 \times 2\pi \times 5 = 20\pi$ (which is equivalent to 0).
$$\Gamma_A = \Gamma_L = -0.5 = 0.50\angle180°$$

## Q7 — VSWR of a Short-Circuited Lossless TL

> A lossless TL is terminated with a **short-circuit**.  
> **What is the voltage standing wave ratio, VSWR, of the total wave in the TL?**

### Theory  
For a short-circuit load, $Z_L = 0$ so
$$
\Gamma = \frac{Z_L - Z_0}{Z_L + Z_0} = \frac{-Z_0}{Z_0} = -1,
$$
so $|\Gamma| = 1$.  

The VSWR is
$$
\text{VSWR} = \frac{1 + |\Gamma|}{1 - |\Gamma|}.
$$
If $|\Gamma| = 1$, denominator is zero and VSWR $\to \infty$.

### Solution
$$\Gamma = -1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad |\Gamma| = 1$$
$$\text{VSWR} = \frac{1 + 1}{1 - 1} = \frac{2}{0} = \infty$$

### Answer Choices  
- [ ] -1  
- [ ] 0  
- [ ] 1  
- [x] ∞ ✓

## Q18 — Classification of EM Field

> $$\mathbf{E} = E_0 \begin{pmatrix} 0\\ j\\ 0 \end{pmatrix} e^{-j(20x+10z)/\text{m}}, \quad \mathbf{H} = H_0 \begin{pmatrix} 0\\ 0\\ j \end{pmatrix} e^{-j(20x+10z)/\text{m}}$$
> where $E_0 = 5\ \text{V/m}$ and $H_0 = \frac{1}{120\pi}\ \text{A/m}$.  
> **What can be said about the wave?**

### Theory  
For a valid **uniform plane wave**, three orthogonality conditions must ALL be satisfied:
1. $\vec{\beta} \cdot \vec{E}_0 = 0$ (E ⊥ k)
2. $\vec{\beta} \cdot \vec{H}_0 = 0$ (H ⊥ k)
3. $\vec{E}_0 \cdot \vec{H}_0 = 0$ (E ⊥ H)

**If any of these conditions are not satisfied, the field does not represent a plane wave.**

### Solution
From the phase term $e^{-j(20x+10z)}$:
$$\vec{k} = 20\hat{x} + 10\hat{z}$$

**Check all three conditions:**

1. $\vec{k} \cdot \vec{E}_0 = (20\hat{x} + 10\hat{z}) \cdot \hat{y} = 0$ ✓
2. $\vec{k} \cdot \vec{H}_0 = (20\hat{x} + 10\hat{z}) \cdot \hat{z} = 10 \neq 0$ ✗ **FAILS!**
3. $\vec{E}_0 \cdot \vec{H}_0 = \hat{y} \cdot \hat{z} = 0$ ✓

**The H-field has a component parallel to the propagation direction**, violating the transversality requirement for plane waves.

### Answer Choices  
- [x] It is not a plane wave ✓
- [ ] It is a uniform plane wave propagating in a lossless medium  
- [ ] It is a non-uniform plane wave propagating in a lossy medium  
- [ ] It is a uniform plane wave propagating in a lossy medium

## Q21 — Polarization of Given H-Field

> Plane wave propagating in $+x$-direction:  
> $$\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 0\\ 10 + j10\\ 10 - j10 \end{pmatrix}\ \frac{\text{mA}}{\text{m}}$$  
> **What is the polarization?**

### Theory  
- Compare magnitudes and phases of transverse components
- Equal magnitudes with 90° phase difference → circular
- Handedness from rotation direction vs propagation

### Solution
$H_y = 10 + j10 = 10\sqrt{2}\angle45°$
