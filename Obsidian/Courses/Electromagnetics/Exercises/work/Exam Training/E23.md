## Q1 — Propagation Constant $\gamma$

Which expression is correct for the propagation constant $\gamma$ of a transmission line with per-unit-length parameters $R', L', G', C'$?

### Theory  
The general propagation constant of a transmission line is:

$$
\gamma = \alpha + j\beta = \sqrt{(R' + j\omega L')(G' + j\omega C')} .
$$

This arises from solving the telegrapher’s equations and includes both conductor losses ($R'$) and dielectric losses ($G'$).

Lossless case:

$$
R' = 0,\quad G' = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \gamma = j\omega\sqrt{L'C'} .
$$

### Answer Choices  
- [ ] $\sqrt{\dfrac{G' + j\omega C'}{R' + j\omega L'}}$  
- [ ] $\sqrt{(R' + j\omega L')(G' + j\omega C')}$  
- [ ] $\sqrt{\dfrac{R' + j\omega L'}{G' + j\omega C'}}$  
- [ ] $\sqrt{(G' + j\omega L')(R' + j\omega C')}$

## Q2 — Phase Velocity Expression

Which expression gives the phase velocity $u_p$ of a wave?

### Theory  
For a wave of the form $\exp(j(\omega t - \beta z))$, the phase is constant when:

$$
\omega t - \beta z = \text{const}.
$$

Taking the derivative gives:

$$
u_p = \frac{\omega}{\beta}.
$$

### Answer Choices  
- [ ] $\dfrac{\partial \beta}{\partial \omega}$  
- [ ] $\omega\beta$  
- [ ] $\beta/\omega$  
- [ ] $\omega/\beta$

## Q7 — TL Length Such That $\Gamma_A = \Gamma_B$

![[Q7png.png]]
Given $\Gamma_B = 0.13\angle 30^\circ$, find TL length $\ell$ such that $\Gamma_A = \Gamma_B$.

### Theory  
Reflection coefficient transforms along a TL as:

$$
\Gamma_A = \Gamma_B e^{-j2\beta \ell}.
$$

To satisfy $\Gamma_A = \Gamma_B$:

$$
e^{-j2\beta \ell} = 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
2\beta\ell = 2\pi n.
$$

Fundamental nonzero solution:

$$
\ell = \frac{\lambda}{2}.
$$

### Answer Choices

- [ ] $\lambda/4$  
- [ ] $\lambda/2$

## Q16 — Transmission Line Length $d$

### Problem Statement

An antenna has an input impedance of $Z_L = (142 + j42.5)\ \Omega$ and shall be matched to a system based on coaxial cables of $Z_0 = 75\ \Omega$.
A single-stub tuner with a short-circuited stub is used.

**What is the correct length $d$ of the transmission line between M and $Z_L$?**

### Given Values

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| $Z_L$ | $(142 + j42.5)\ \Omega$ |
| $Z_0$ | $75\ \Omega$ |
| $\lambda$ | $13.3\ \text{cm}$ (from Q15) |

### Theory

1. Normalize load:
$$
z_L = \frac{Z_L}{Z_0}
$$

2. Convert to admittance:
$$
y_L = \frac{1}{z_L} = g_L + jb_L
$$

3. Solve for $t = \tan(\beta d)$ to reach $g = 1$ circle:
$$
t^2(g_L - b_L^2 - g_L^2) + 2b_L t + (g_L - 1) = 0
$$

4. Convert electrical length to physical:
$$
d = \frac{\arctan(t)}{360°} \times \lambda
$$

### Solution

$$z_L = \frac{142 + j42.5}{75} = 1.893 + j0.567$$

$$y_L = \frac{1}{1.893 + j0.567} = 0.485 - j0.145$$

Solving quadratic: $t = 2.264 \Rightarrow \beta d = 66.15°$

$$d = \frac{66.15°}{360°} \times 13.3\ \text{cm} = 2.45\ \text{cm} = 24.5\ \text{mm}$$

### Answer Choices

- [ ] $11.7\ \text{mm}$  
- [x] $24.5\ \text{mm}$ 
- [ ] $35.5\ \text{mm}$  
- [ ] $16.9\ \text{mm}$

## Q17 — Stub Length $\ell$

### Problem Statement

An antenna has an input impedance of $Z_L = (142 + j42.5)\ \Omega$ and shall be matched to a system based on coaxial cables of $Z_0 = 75\ \Omega$.
A single-stub tuner with a **short-circuited stub** is used.

**What is the correct length $\ell$ of the stub?**


### Given Values

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| $Z_L$ | $(142 + j42.5)\ \Omega$ |
| $Z_0$ | $75\ \Omega$ |
| $\lambda$ | $13.3\ \text{cm}$ (from Q15) |
| $d$ | $24.5\ \text{mm}$ (from Q16) |

### Theory

At match point:
$$
y_M = 1 + jb_M
$$

For a short-circuited stub:
$$
Z_{\text{stub}} = jZ_0 \tan(\beta\ell)
$$

Admittance:
$$
y_{\text{stub}} = -j\cot(\beta\ell)
$$

Matching condition (susceptance cancellation):
$$
b_M + b_{\text{stub}} = 0 \implies \cot(\beta\ell) = b_M
$$

### Solution

From Q16 transformation: $y_M = 1 + j0.768$

Stub requirement: $y_{\text{stub}} = -j0.768$

$$\cot(\beta\ell) = 0.768 \implies \tan(\beta\ell) = 1.302$$

$$\beta\ell = \arctan(1.302) = 52.45°$$

$$\ell = \frac{52.45°}{360°} \times 13.3\ \text{cm} = 1.94\ \text{cm} = 19.4\ \text{mm}$$

### Answer Choices

- [x] $19.4\ \text{mm}$ 
- [ ] $28.2\ \text{mm}$  
- [ ] $13.4\ \text{mm}$  
- [ ] $9.24\ \text{mm}$

## Q18 — Behaviour of the EM Wave

### Problem Statement

A plane electromagnetic wave has the magnetic field:

$$
\tilde{H}(\vec{r},t) = H_0 \left[ \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \cos(\omega t - \vec{\beta}\cdot\vec{r}) - \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \sin(\omega t - \vec{\beta}\cdot\vec{r}) \right] e^{-\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{r}}
$$

**Given Values:**

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| $H_0$ | $0.01\ \text{A/m}$ |
| $\tilde{\gamma} = \tilde{\alpha} + j\tilde{\beta}$ | $\begin{pmatrix} 0.5 \\ 0 \\ 1+j20 \end{pmatrix}\ \text{m}^{-1}$ |

**Which one of the following statements is correct?**
### Theory

Extract $\vec{\alpha}$ and $\vec{\beta}$ from $\tilde{\gamma}$:

$$
\vec{\alpha} = \text{Re}(\tilde{\gamma}) = \begin{pmatrix} 0.5 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}\ \text{m}^{-1}, \quad
\vec{\beta} = \text{Im}(\tilde{\gamma}) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 20 \end{pmatrix}\ \text{m}^{-1}
$$

**Key test:** Are $\vec{\alpha}$ and $\vec{\beta}$ parallel?

$$
\vec{\alpha} \times \vec{\beta} = \begin{pmatrix} 0.5 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \times \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 20 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -10 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \neq \vec{0}
$$

**Not parallel** → **Non-uniform plane wave**

### Wave Classification

| Wave Type | Condition | Magnitude | Phase |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Uniform plane wave | $\vec{\alpha} \parallel \vec{\beta}$ | Constant in planes ⊥ to propagation | Constant in planes ⊥ to propagation |
| Non-uniform plane wave | $\vec{\alpha} \not\parallel \vec{\beta}$ | **Not constant** | **Not constant** |

### Solution

Since $\vec{\alpha} \not\parallel \vec{\beta}$:
- The term $e^{-\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{r}}$ causes **magnitude variation** in space
- The term $(\omega t - \vec{\beta}\cdot\vec{r})$ causes **phase variation** in space
- The attenuation is in a different direction than propagation

**The wave is a non-uniform plane wave** → Both magnitude and phase vary in space.

### Answer Choices

- [x] The magnitude and phase of the electric field are not constant in all space ✓
- [ ] The wave is a uniform plane wave
- [ ] The magnitude of the electric field is constant in all space
- [ ] The phase of the electric field is constant in all space

## Q20 — Normal Vector to Equiphase Surfaces

### Problem Statement

From Q18, the electromagnetic wave has propagation constant: $$ \tilde{\gamma} = \vec{\alpha} + j\vec{\beta} = \begin{pmatrix} 0.5 \ 0 \ 1+j20 \end{pmatrix}\ \text{m}^{-1} $$

|Vector|Value|
|---|---|
|$\vec{\alpha}$|$(0.5,\ 0,\ 1)\ \text{m}^{-1}$|
|$\vec{\beta}$|$(0,\ 0,\ 20)\ \text{m}^{-1}$|

### Theory

The phase of the wave is determined by: $$ \phi = \omega t - \vec{\beta} \cdot \vec{r} $$

**Equiphase surfaces** are defined by $\vec{\beta} \cdot \vec{r} = \text{constant}$

The **normal vector** to these surfaces is parallel to $\vec{\beta}$: $$ \hat{n} = \frac{\vec{\beta}}{|\vec{\beta}|} $$

### Solution

$$ \vec{\beta} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \ 0 \ 20 \end{pmatrix}\ \text{m}^{-1} $$

$$ |\vec{\beta}| = \sqrt{0^2 + 0^2 + 20^2} = 20\ \text{m}^{-1} $$

$$ \hat{n} = \frac{1}{20} \begin{pmatrix} 0 \ 0 \ 20 \end{pmatrix} = \boxed{\begin{pmatrix} 0 \ 0 \ 1 \end{pmatrix}} = \hat{z} $$

### Key Concept

|Surface Type|Determined By|Normal Vector|
|---|---|---|
|**Equiphase** (constant phase)|$\vec{\beta} \cdot \vec{r} = \text{const}$|$\hat{n} \parallel \vec{\beta}$|
|Equiamplitude (constant amplitude)|$\vec{\alpha} \cdot \vec{r} = \text{const}$|$\hat{n} \parallel \vec{\alpha}$|

### Answer Choices

- [ ] $\hat{n} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \ 0 \ 0 \end{pmatrix}$
- [ ] $\hat{n} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \ 1 \ 0 \end{pmatrix}$

- [ ] $\hat{n} = 0.05\begin{pmatrix} 0.5 \ 0 \ 21 \end{pmatrix}$

**Answer: $\hat{n} = (0, 0, 1) = \hat{z}$** ✓

## Q21 — Frequency of a Given Plane Wave

### Problem Statement

A plane electromagnetic wave is propagating in air and has the electric field:

$$ \vec{E}(\vec{r},t) = E_0 \begin{pmatrix} 2 \ 1 \ 0 \end{pmatrix} \cos(\omega t + [4y - 2x - 2z]/\text{m}) + E_0 \begin{pmatrix} 0 \ -1 \ -2 \end{pmatrix} \sin(\omega t + [4y - 2x - 2z]/\text{m}) $$

**Given Values:**

|Parameter|Value|
|---|---|
|$E_0$|$10\ \text{V/m}$|
|Medium|Air ($\approx$ vacuum)|

**What is the frequency of the wave?**

### Theory

The phase of a plane wave is: $$ \phi = \omega t - \vec{\beta} \cdot \vec{r} = \omega t - (\beta_x x + \beta_y y + \beta_z z) $$

From the given expression $\omega t + (4y - 2x - 2z)$, we identify: $$ \vec{\beta} = (2,\ -4,\ 2)\ \text{rad/m} $$

In a lossless medium (air): $$ \beta = \frac{\omega}{c_0} \implies f = \frac{\beta \cdot c_0}{2\pi} $$

### Solution

**Step 1: Find $|\vec{\beta}|$** $$ |\vec{\beta}| = \sqrt{2^2 + (-4)^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{4 + 16 + 4} = \sqrt{24} = 4.899\ \text{rad/m} $$

**Step 2: Calculate frequency** $$ f = \frac{\beta \cdot c_0}{2\pi} = \frac{4.899 \times 2.998 \times 10^8}{2\pi} = \frac{1.469 \times 10^9}{2\pi} = 2.338 \times 10^8\ \text{Hz} $$

$$ \boxed{f \approx 234\ \text{MHz}} $$
### Answer Choices

- [ ] $1.15\ \text{GHz}$
- [ ] $47.7\ \text{MHz}$
- [ ] $1.47\ \text{GHz}$
- [x] $234\ \text{MHz}$ ✓

### Theory

For a linear isotropic medium with conductivity $\sigma$ and permittivity $\varepsilon = \varepsilon_r\varepsilon_0$, the **loss tangent** is:

$$ \tan\delta = \frac{\sigma}{\omega\varepsilon} = \frac{\sigma}{2\pi f \varepsilon_r\varepsilon_0} $$

**Manual Calculation:**

$$ \tan\delta = \frac{0.01}{2\pi \times 5\times10^9 \times 8 \times 8.854\times10^{-12}} = \frac{0.01}{2.224} \approx 4.5 \times 10^{-3} $$

**Classification Heuristics:**

- $\tan\delta \ll 1$ (< 0.1) → **Low-Loss Dielectric** (good insulator)
- $\tan\delta \gg 1$ (> 10) → **Good Conductor**
- $0.1 < \tan\delta < 10$ → **Quasi-Conductor**

**Idealized Limits:**

- Perfect electric conductor: $\sigma \to \infty$
- Perfect dielectric: $\sigma = 0$ (or $\tan\delta = 0$)

**Result:**  
Since $\tan\delta = 0.0045 \ll 1$, the material behaves as a **good insulator** (lossy dielectric with small loss).

- **Wavelength** ≈ 1.4 cm (millimeter wave range)
- **Phase velocity** ≈ 0.47c (47% speed of light due to εᵣ = 8)
- **Attenuation** ≈ 1 Np/m means signal drops to 37% after 1 meter
- **Impedance** ≈ 133 Ω (mostly resistive, very small reactive component)

The displacement current ($j\omega\varepsilon E$) dominates over the conduction current ($\sigma E$) by a factor of ~222, confirming dielectric behavior.

## Q32 — Reflected Power Fraction

For the same incidence as above, how much of the incident power is reflected?

### Theory  

For each polarization we use the Fresnel coefficients:

- TE:

$$
\Gamma_{\text{TE}} = 
\frac{ \eta_2 \cos\theta_i - \eta_1 \cos\theta_t }
     { \eta_2 \cos\theta_i + \eta_1 \cos\theta_t },
$$

- TM:

$$
\Gamma_{\text{TM}} =
\frac{ \eta_2 \cos\theta_t - \eta_1 \cos\theta_i }
     { \eta_2 \cos\theta_t + \eta_1 \cos\theta_i },
$$

where $\eta_k$ is the intrinsic impedance of medium $k$.  
The **power reflectance** for each polarization is

$$
R_{\text{TE}} = |\Gamma_{\text{TE}}|^2,\qquad
R_{\text{TM}} = |\Gamma_{\text{TM}}|^2.
$$

Because the incident wave is elliptically polarized (a mix of TE and TM), its power is split between TE and TM components. The **total reflected power fraction** is the weighted sum of the two reflectances, based on how much of the incident field is TE vs TM.

### Answer Choices  

- [ ] $11.3\ \%$  
- [ ] $22.6\ \%$  
- [ ] $8\ \%$  
- [x] $14.6\ \%$
