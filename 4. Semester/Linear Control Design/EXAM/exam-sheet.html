34722 Linear Control Design 1 — oral exam sheet  front: the numbers

1 · Second-order system — everything starts here

G(s) = ωn² / (s² + 2ζωns + ωn²)
poles s = −ζωn ± jωd,   ωd = ωn√(1−ζ²)

Read the pole picture: ωn = distance from origin (speed). θ from the negative real axis = shape, with cosθ = ζ. Real part −ζωn kills the wobble; imaginary part ωd is the wobble.

ωn only rescales the time axis. ζ alone sets the shape. Move the poles round the circle and overshoot changes; move them along the radius and only the clock changes.

2 · Overshoot ↔ damping

Mp = e−ζπ/√(1−ζ²)    ζ = |ln Mp| / √(π² + ln²Mp)
ζ0.30.40.50.60.70.81.0
Mp37%25%16%9.5%4.6%1.5%0

Own the three bold pairs and interpolate the rest out loud. ζ = 1 is critically damped: double real pole, ωd = 0, no overshoot at all.

Why ωn vanishes: the first peak is at tp = π/ωd; substitute into e−ζωnt and ωn cancels top and bottom.

3 · Time-domain landmarks

tp = π/ωd    ts ≈ 4/(ζωn) [2%]    3/(ζωn) [5%]    tr ≈ 1.8/ωn

The 4 is just ln 50 — the envelope e−ζωnt has decayed to 2%. For 5% it is ln 20 ≈ 3.

ts has ζωn underneath, not ωn. The dropped ζ is the classic marked-wrong.

Two-knob design: overshoot spec → ζ. Then speed spec (ts or tp) → ωn. Never both at once.

4 · Frequency landmarks

  • Gain crossover: ωc = ωn√(√(1+4ζ4) − 2ζ²)
  • Bandwidth: ωBW = ωn√((1−2ζ²) + √(4ζ4−4ζ²+2))
  • Resonance (only ζ < 0.707): ωr = ωn√(1−2ζ²), Mr = 1/(2ζ√(1−ζ²))
  • ωc < ωBW ≈ (1.0–1.5)ωn
ζ4, not ζ or ζ². Two of the inherited MATLAB helpers have this typo — these forms are the corrected ones.

5 · Phase margin ↔ damping

γM = arctan( 2ζ / √(√(1+4ζ4) − 2ζ²) )
Rule of thumb: γM[°] ≈ 100ζ for ζ ≲ 0.6. So PM 45° → ζ ≈ 0.45 → Mp ≈ 20%. This single line links the frequency domain to the step response — say it aloud.

6 · Steady-state error (unity feedback)

E = R/(1+L)  ⇒   ess = lims→0 s·R(s)/(1+L(s))
Typesteprampparabola
01/(1+Kp)∞∞
101/Kv∞
2001/Ka

Kp = lims→0 L, Kv = lims→0 sL, Ka = lims→0 s²L. Type = number of integrators in L.

Derive it, don't recite it. Unit step R = 1/s, so ess = 1/(1+L(0)). Type 0 → L(0) finite → error finite and never zero. Type 1 → L(0) = ∞ → error zero.

Scaled step of size A: multiply the whole result by A. A 30-unit step with L(0) = 0.03 gives 30/(1.03) ≈ 29.1 — not 0.97.

7 · Sensitivity S and T

S = 1/(1+L) = e/r     T = L/(1+L) = y/r     S + T = 1
Why it must be 1: with unity feedback the junction defines e = r − y. Divide by r: e/r = 1 − y/r, i.e. S = 1 − T. Got-it and missing-it are the only two options.

Design meaning: small S = accurate tracking & disturbance rejection. Small T = noise rejected & robust. They sum to 1, so you cannot win both at one frequency — buy S at low ω (high |L|) and T at high ω (low |L|). That is loop shaping.

Needs unity feedback. With a sensor H the junction gives e = r − Hy, and S + T = 1 no longer holds.

8 · Disturbances

Denominator never changes — only the numerator does. The numerator is whatever sits between the injection point and the output.
D entersY/D
at the output1/(1+L)
at the plant inputG2/(1+L)
before the controllerG1G2/(1+L)

Entering early is worst — it gets the full forward gain, exactly like the reference. Rejection is complete only where |L| → ∞, i.e. an integrator upstream of the entry point.

9 · Transform theorems

  • Final value: y(∞) = lims→0 sY(s) — only if all poles of sY(s) are in the open LHP (one pole at the origin allowed).
  • Initial value: y(0+) = lims→∞ sY(s).
Shortcut: Y strictly proper (den degree > num degree) ⇒ y(0+) = 0.
On an unstable or purely oscillating signal the FVT still returns a number. It is meaningless. Always say the validity condition aloud before using it.

10 · Block reduction — the whole toolkit

Series → G₁G₂
One feeds the next: multiply. Nothing enters a denominator.
Parallel → G₁ + G₂
Same input, outputs meet at a junction: add, with each branch's sign. A subtracting junction gives G₁ − G₂.
Feedback → G/(1 ± GH)
+ for a negative junction, − for a positive one. Loop gain = everything met going once around.
In front of the loop → outside it
AB/(1+BH). The wire returns after A, so A never enters the denominator.
After the takeoff → outside it
G₁G₂/(1+G₁H). G₂ is downstream of where the wire leaves, so it is in the numerator only.
Two taps summed → B/(1 + B(C+D))
The June Q6 shape. Both are fed by the same output and meet at a junction, so they add. Never BCD.

The rest, in words

  • Nested loops: innermost first, then treat it as one block. Multiply the compound fraction out: G₁G₂/(1 + G₂H₂ + G₁G₂H₁).
  • Independent loops (sharing no block): close each, multiply. Denominators factor only here.
  • Series in the feedback path: H₁H₂ multiply, same as anywhere else.
  • Feedforward: a branch leaving the input and rejoining downstream is parallel — it changes the numerator, never the denominator.
Clearing a compound fraction: multiply top and bottom by the inner denominator. The standalone 1 picks it up too. Do not cancel — cancelling needs the factor under every term.

11 · Nyquist & margins

Z = N + P

Z = closed-loop RHP poles (need 0). N = clockwise encirclements of −1. P = open-loop RHP poles. Stable ⇔ N = −P.

  • Stable plant (P = 0): need no encirclement. Crossing at −x ⇒ stable for K < 1/x.
  • One RHP pole (P = 1): need one counter-clockwise encirclement, which only happens once the crossing passes −1 ⇒ stable for K > 1/x.
The interval inverts for an unstable plant. And the boundary value itself is only marginal — pick a value strictly inside, never K = 1/x.

Margins: GM = 1/|G(jωpc)| where ∠G = −180°; γM = 180° + ∠G(jωgc) where |G| = 1. Physically: how much extra gain, or extra lag, before the loop starts oscillating.

Relative degree ≥ 3 ⇒ phase runs past −180° while the magnitude is still falling, so a large enough P-gain always destabilises it.

12 · PI-Lead design

−180° + γM = ∠G(jωc) + φLead + φPI

∠G is what the plant already gives (read it off). The lead pays in, the PI costs.

  • Lead CD = (τds + 1)/(ατds + 1), α < 1. Zero first, then pole — that is what makes the phase positive.
  • φmax = arcsin((1−α)/(1+α)) at ω = 1/(τd√α), where |CD| = 1/√α.
  • Need +20 dB from the lead ⇒ 1/√α = 10 ⇒ α = 0.01. Place zero at ωc√α, pole at ωc/√α, crossover at their geometric mean.
  • PI costs phase: φPI = arctan(Ni) − 90°. That is the price of killing steady-state error.
  • Gain last: KP·|CD|·|CPI|·|G| = 1 at ωc.
Pure P for a target PM — two steps. (1) Phase pins the crossover: K moves no phase, so ωc must sit where ∠G = −180° + γM. (2) Gain sets it: KP = 1/|G(jωc)|.

Reversed (pole first) it is a lag: −20 dB and negative phase. Lag/PI buys accuracy, lead buys margin and speed.

13 · Trap checklist — read before answering

□ Summed ≠ cascaded. Taps meeting at a junction add (C+D); one feeding the next multiplies (CD).
□ Never drop the 1. The denominator is 1 + L, not L.
□ Trace the loop before writing it. Blocks ahead of the junction or after the takeoff are outside.
□ Read every junction sign — each loop keeps its own.
□ Multiply through, never cancel when clearing a compound fraction.
□ ts = 4/(ζωn), and ζ4 in the ωc/ωBW formulas.
□ RHP plant inverts the Nyquist K-interval; a marginal value is not a stable one.
□ Reciprocals: GM = 1/|G|, ζ from Mp not Mp from ζ — check which way round they asked.
□ dB vs linear: convert first (−8 dB → 0.4), then do the algebra.
□ Scaled step: multiply ess by the step size.
□ Non-physical signs — a settling time or ζ cannot be negative. If it is, you took a wrong root.

14 · Say these out loud

“ωn scales the time axis, ζ sets the shape — that is why ωn cancels out of Mp.”
“The denominator is 1 plus the loop gain, and the loop gain is everything I meet going once around the loop.”
“Innermost loop first, then I treat it as a single block.”
“S + T = 1 because the junction defines the error as reference minus output.”
“Let me sanity-check: set H = 0 and the expression should collapse to the plain loop — it does.”
“The FVT is only valid because sY(s) has all its poles in the left half-plane.”

If you get stuck: say what you do know — name the structure, name the rule, write the general form, then substitute. Examiners mark the reasoning, and a narrated method scores where a silent wrong number does not.
