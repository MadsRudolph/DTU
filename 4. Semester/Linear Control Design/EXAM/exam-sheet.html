1 · Block diagram reduction

SAY: “Forward path over one plus the loop gain — and the loop gain is everything I meet going once around the loop.”
  • Series → multiply. Parallel (summed at a junction) → add.
  • Negative feedback G/(1+GH) · positive G/(1−GH).
  • Nested → innermost first, then treat the result as one block.
  • Blocks in front of the junction or after the takeoff are OUTSIDE the loop → numerator only.
  • Two taps summed → (C+D), never CD.
  • Disturbance: denominator never changes; numerator = what sits between the entry point and the output.
Never drop the 1. Clearing a compound fraction: multiply through, don't cancel — the standalone 1 gets multiplied too. A summing junction between two blocks does not stop G₁·G₂ for the R→Y path.
Check: set H = 0 and the expression must collapse to the plain loop. Say this aloud — it catches sign slips.

Why ωn cancels: the peak is at tp = π/ωd; substitute that into e−ζωnt and ωn cancels top and bottom. So overshoot is the pole angle alone.
ts = 4/(ζωn) — the ζ is in there. And ζ4 (not ζ²) in the ωc/ωBW formulas.

Two knobs: overshoot spec → ζ, then speed spec → ωn. Never both at once.
ωc = ωn√(√(1+4ζ4)−2ζ²) · ωBW = ωn√((1−2ζ²)+√(4ζ4−4ζ²+2))

3 · Steady-state error

SAY: “I start from E = R over one plus L, apply the final value theorem, then count integrators.”
ess = lims→0 s·R(s)/(1+L(s))  →  step: 1/(1+L(0))

Kp = lim L, Kv = lim sL, Ka = lim s²L as s→0. Type = integrators in L.

Thermostat story: a proportional heater needs a gap to keep working, so a gap always remains. An integrator keeps accumulating and cannot stop until the error is exactly zero — it has no other resting point.
Multiply by the step size if it isn't 1. Convert dB → linear first (−8 dB → 0.4), then do algebra.

4 · Stability & margins

SAY: “Both margins ask the same question — how far is the loop from the minus-one point — one measured in gain, one in phase.”
  • PM = 180° + ∠G, measured where |G| = 1
  • GM = 1/|G|, measured where ∠G = −180°
Shower story: long pipe = delay. You turn it up, nothing happens, you turn it up more, it scalds, you overcorrect — oscillation. Phase margin = how much more delay before that starts.
Why −180° is fatal: negative feedback already flips the sign; another 180° of lag makes the returning signal reinforce instead of cancel. If the gain is 1 or more there, it grows without bound.

At exactly 0 dB and −180° → marginal: constant-amplitude oscillation, the tipping point. Never design there.

5 · Nyquist

Z = N + P  →  stable ⇔ N = −P

Z = closed-loop RHP poles (need 0). N = clockwise encirclements of −1. P = open-loop RHP poles.

  • Stable plant (P = 0): no encirclement → crossing at −x gives K < 1/x
  • One RHP pole (P = 1): need one counter-clockwise → K > 1/x
The interval INVERTS for an unstable plant, and the boundary value itself is only marginal — pick strictly inside, never K = 1/x.
Broom story: balancing a broom on your palm. React gently and it falls. Unstable plants need aggressive control — that's why the inequality flips.

Relative degree ≥ 3 → phase runs past −180° while the gain is still above 1, so a large enough P-gain always destabilises it.

6 · Bode plots

SAY: “It's just how the system responds to slow wiggles versus fast ones — magnitude is how much it moves, phase is how far behind it lags.”
Factormagnitudephase
pole at a−20 dB/dec after a→ −90°
zero at a+20 dB/dec after a→ +90°
integrator−20 dB/dec everywhereflat −90°

System type from the plot: low-frequency phase start — 0° = type 0, −90° = type 1, −180° = type 2. (Or low-frequency slope ÷ −20.)

Integrator signature: the magnitude never flattens out at low frequency.

RHP zero: magnitude rises like a normal zero but phase falls like a pole. That mismatch is the non-minimum-phase fingerprint.
Bike story: to turn right you first steer left. The system moves the wrong way first, so naive aggressive control fights it and goes unstable.

Reading one backwards: flat at 20 dB → DC gain 10, no integrator; breaks down at ω = 5 → one pole at 5. So G = 10/(s/5 + 1).

7 · PI-Lead design

SAY: “It's a phase budget. The plant gives me what it gives me, the lead pays in, and the PI costs.”
−180° + γM = ∠G(jωc) + φLead + φPI
  • Lead (τs+1)/(ατs+1), α < 1, zero first then pole — that ordering is what makes the phase positive.
  • φmax = arcsin((1−α)/(1+α)) at ω = 1/(τ√α), where |CD| = 1/√α.
  • Need +20 dB → 1/√α = 10 → α = 0.01. Zero at ωc√α, pole at ωc/√α, crossover at their geometric mean.
  • PI costs phase: φPI = arctan(Ni) − 90° — the price of killing steady-state error.
  • Gain last: KP·|CD|·|CPI|·|G| = 1 at ωc.
P-only, two steps: (1) phase pins the crossover — find ω where ∠G = −180° + γM. (2) gain sets it — KP = 1/|G(jωc)|.
Lead = anticipation. A driver who eases off early because they see they're closing on a red light, instead of braking on arrival. Anticipation is the opposite of delay, so it buys back the margin delay stole.

Reversed (pole first) it's a lag: −20 dB and negative phase. Lag/PI buys accuracy; lead buys margin and speed.

8 · Sensitivity S and T

SAY: “With unity feedback the junction defines e = r − y. Divide through by r and you get S = 1 − T.”
S = 1/(1+L) = e/r    T = L/(1+L) = y/r    S + T = 1
Pie story: you asked for 100, got 97, missing 3. Got-it and missing-it are the only two options, so they add to the whole.

Design consequence: small S = accurate tracking and disturbance rejection; small T = noise rejected and robust. They sum to 1, so you cannot win both at one frequency — buy S at low ω, T at high ω. That is loop shaping.

Only true for unity feedback. With a sensor H the junction gives e = r − Hy and the identity breaks.

ess = 1 − T(0) for a step is this same identity, unnamed.

9 · Transform theorems

  • FVT: y(∞) = lims→0 sY(s) — valid only if all poles of sY(s) are in the open LHP (one at the origin allowed).
  • IVT: y(0+) = lims→∞ sY(s).
  • Shortcut: Y strictly proper ⇒ y(0+) = 0.
SAY: “The final value theorem is valid here because all poles of sY(s) are in the left half-plane.” Saying the condition earns the mark.

10 · Before you commit to any answer

□ Did I add the summed taps, not multiply them?
□ Is the 1 still in the denominator?
□ Did I trace which blocks the loop actually encircles?
□ Did I read every junction sign?
□ Did I multiply through rather than cancel?
□ Is ζ under the settling time?
□ Unstable plant → did the K-interval flip?
□ dB converted to linear before algebra?
□ Reciprocal check — which way round did they ask?
□ Any non-physical sign? ζ and ts cannot be negative.

11 · If you get stuck

Name the structure → name the rule → write the general form → then substitute. Examiners mark the reasoning, and a narrated method scores where a silent wrong number does not.

Two lines that always buy credit:

“Let me sanity-check — if I set H to zero this should collapse to the plain loop, and it does.”
“Before I use the final value theorem, let me confirm the poles are all in the left half-plane.”

The five stories: suspension = damping · thermostat = steady-state error · shower with a long pipe = phase margin · broom on your palm = unstable plant needs aggressive control · countersteering a bike = RHP zero.
